Asthma is a common chronic inflammatory disease of the airways. It is characterized by variable and recurring symptoms, reversible airflow obstruction and bronchospasm. Common symptoms include wheezing, coughing, chest tightness and shortness of breath. Asthma is thought to be caused by a combination of genetic and environmental factors. Environmental factors include exposure to air pollution and allergens.

Inhaled corticosteroids are the most effective long-term medication for asthma. Short-acting beta agonists such as salbutamol are used to treat acute symptoms. Asthma can be classified as atopic or non-atopic. Atopic asthma is associated with a personal or family history of eczema, allergic rhinitis and allergic conjunctivitis. Tobacco smoking and obesity are risk factors for asthma.

Asthma is a risk factor for chronic obstructive pulmonary disease in some people. Viral respiratory infections are a common trigger of asthma attacks. Avoidance of allergens and tobacco smoke can prevent symptoms. Severe asthma may require oral corticosteroids and close medical monitoring.
